Introduction

In this homework, I use the customer satisfaction survey data to compare different variable importance methods. The response variable is satisfaction, and the predictors are the perception variables about the credit card.

I did not use brand or id in the driver model. id is only an identifier, and brand is not one of the perception statements. The main goal is to see which perceptions are most strongly related to customer satisfaction.

The methods I use are Pearson correlations, standardized regression coefficients, drop-one usefulness, Shapley values, Johnson’s relative weights, and random forest mean decrease in Gini. I omit polychoric correlations because they are optional in the assignment.

Load the data

(2553, 12)

The data has 2,553 rows and 12 columns. I use satisfaction as the outcome variable and the nine perception variables as predictors.

Variable importance calculations

To make the methods easier to compare, I converted every method into percentages that sum to 100.

Pearson correlations

Pearson correlation is the simplest method. It looks at the relationship between each predictor and satisfaction one at a time.

Standardized regression coefficients

For this method, I standardized the predictors and the outcome first. Then I fit a linear regression model and used the absolute values of the coefficients.

Drop-one usefulness

Drop-one usefulness checks how much the full model’s R-squared drops when one variable is removed.

Shapley values

Shapley values split the model R-squared across variables. For each variable, I calculate its average added contribution across all possible model combinations.

Johnson’s relative weights

Johnson’s relative weights are another way to split explained variance across predictors. This method is useful because predictors in survey data are often correlated with each other.

Random forest mean decrease in Gini

For the random forest method, I treat satisfaction as a classification target. The importance scores come from the decrease in Gini impurity.

Interpretation

The results are not exactly the same across all methods, but the main pattern is still pretty clear. The strongest drivers are usually impact, trust, and service. These variables are about whether the card makes a difference in the customer’s life, whether the customer trusts the brand, and whether the card provides strong customer service.

Pearson correlation is easy to read, but it looks at each predictor separately. Because of that, it does not fully adjust for overlap between the perception variables.

The regression-based methods are more useful for looking at unique contribution. In the standardized coefficient and drop-one usefulness columns, impact and trust become especially important. This means they explain more unique variation in satisfaction after the other variables are already included.

The Shapley values and Johnson’s relative weights are my preferred results here. They divide the model’s explained variance across the variables in a more balanced way. These two methods also give similar results, so I think the conclusion is more stable.

The random forest column is flatter than the regression-based columns. I think this may happen because the predictors are binary variables, so the tree model has less detail to split on. I would not use the random forest column alone for the final business conclusion, but it is still useful as another comparison.

Overall, if the company wants to improve satisfaction, the most important areas to focus on are making the card feel meaningful in customers’ lives, building trust in the brand, and improving customer service.
